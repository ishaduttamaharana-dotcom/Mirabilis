# Brand Voice

## Principles

- **Intentional** — every sentence earns its place; no filler adjectives.
- **Cinematic** — favors sensory, concrete detail (light, hour, texture) over
  abstract claims.
- **Warm** — professional, never cold or corporate.
- **Precise** — specific over generic ("a golden-hour pass and a night-light
  pass", not "great lighting").
- **Confident, not inflated** — no superlatives without a specific reason behind
  them; no unverifiable claims ("best in Nagpur", "#1 studio").

## Approved wording patterns

- Lead with the concrete detail, not the category: "Blue-hour exteriors and warm
  interior sets for a monsoon campaign" > "We did a great resort shoot."
- Short, declarative headlines: "One space. Two personalities." / "Calm on set.
  Ruthless in prep."
- Process language stays plain and sequential (Listen → Design the shoot → Film →
  Deliver).

## Words to avoid

- "Best", "#1", "world-class", "revolutionary" — unverifiable superlatives.
- "Synergy", "solutions", "leverage" — corporate filler, off-voice.
- Exclamation points — the brand doesn't shout.
- Vague scale claims ("hundreds of projects") unless a real, admin-entered number
  backs it (use the `statistics` collection for that, not copy).

## Headlines

- Keep to one clean idea, under ~10 words where possible.
- Sentence case with a full stop for declarative headlines (matches existing
  copy: "Golden hour and after dark, in one story.").

## Case studies (work items)

- Structure: what made the space distinct → what was planned around light →
  what was delivered. Never invent a client quote — testimonials are a separate,
  explicitly-sourced collection, never fabricated to accompany a case study.

## Alt text

- Factual, one sentence, describes what's visible (setting, time of day, subject)
  — not a marketing tagline. Example already in use: "Boutique hillside resort at
  blue hour with mist rolling through the valley."

## Error messages / form labels

- Plain and specific: "Enter a valid email address" not "Invalid input."
- Never blame the user; describe what's needed to proceed.
